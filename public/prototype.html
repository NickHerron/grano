Four Generations of Illinois Grain

Harold and Janie Wilken started milling flour on their Ashkum, Illinois farm in 2015 after decades of growing grain for commodity markets. What started as an experiment with a small stone mill has grown into one of the Midwest's most beloved specialty flour operations.

Their daughter Jennie now runs the day-to-day operation, working directly with Chicago's best bakeries and restaurants to grow and mill the varieties they want — from ancient einkorn to modern bread wheats. Everything is grown on the farm's own soil, milled within days of harvest, and shipped the same week.

"We wanted chefs to taste the difference between flour that's been sitting in a warehouse for a year and flour that was still grain a week ago," Jennie says. "Once they taste it, they never go back."

Growing Practices

Janie's Mill farms 700 acres in Iroquois County using regenerative practices developed over four generations. No synthetic inputs. Soil health is everything.

About these strawberries

Smith Family Farm has grown strawberries on their Elgin property for three generations. Their June-bearing variety — a Chandler cross developed by the family — produces larger, deeper-red berries than commercial varieties, with a fragrance you can smell from across a kitchen. No preservatives, no wax coating, no gas ripening.
